THE QUESTION

Where is performance
off plan—and why?

Retail leaders need to know where performance is ahead of or behind plan, whether forecasts are reliable enough for operational use, and which stores and departments require attention.

This project builds that reporting workflow from raw competition data through a dimensional model, reconciliation workbook, and four-page Power BI report.

01 / PREPARE

Get the data straight.

Python joins weekly sales, store attributes and economic features; standardizes dates and keys; and checks grain and completeness. Negative sales are retained because they may represent returns or accounting adjustments.

02 / MODEL

Make the logic traceable.

SQL reproduces the reporting layer and validation checks. Power BI connects weekly sales to store, department and date dimensions in a star schema.

03 / TEST

Check before presenting.

The Excel model independently reconciles headline totals. Forecasts use only records available before each evaluated period, with holiday errors weighted five times ordinary weeks.

NOT JUST A DASHBOARD

A planning model
you can interrogate.

The interactive workbook switches between Base, Upside and Downside scenarios. Formula-driven summaries update monthly, store and department results, while reconciliation controls check totals, assumptions, scenario validity and source completeness.

WHAT THE ANALYSIS FOUND

The useful part is
knowing where to look.

  • Actual sales were $9.0M below the illustrative plan. The −0.4% headline variance is supported by independent reconciliation.
  • Performance was not uniform. Stores 39, 41 and 4 had the largest favorable plan variances; stores 14, 27 and 36 had the largest unfavorable variances.
  • The simpler forecast performed better. Seasonal-naive forecasting beat the tested gradient-boosting model on weighted error, achieving $1,818 holiday-weighted MAE.

A complete workflow—not a claim of business impact at Walmart.
